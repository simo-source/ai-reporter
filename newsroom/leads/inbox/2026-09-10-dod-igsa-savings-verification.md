# Lead: DOD intergovernmental-agreement savings verification

## Scores

- Public interest: 5/5
- Originality: 3/5
- Document-nativeness: 5/5
- Room to grow: 4/5
- Legal risk (subtracted): 1/5
- Net: 16/25
- Score note: GAO's new review can be compared with the governing statute and DOD's support-agreement instruction. Originality is capped at 3 until agreement-level or certification records test whether the statutory savings and recapture process is observable in practice.

## Hypothesis

DOD's rapid expansion of intergovernmental support agreements may have outpaced the records needed to verify realized savings and calculate the amounts that Congress required the services to return to installations for sustainment, restoration, and modernization.

## What would be new

The finding would map the statutory cost-savings certification and recapture requirements against service-level guidance and public agreement, task-order, or certification records, showing whether the claimed savings can be independently tied to the required installation-level amounts.

## Human stake

The records govern how public money is spent on installation services and whether verified savings return to local military installations for maintenance and modernization.

## Why it might be wrong

The GAO findings may reflect a limited, nongeneralizable sample, while service records not yet located may document the required certifications and actual savings more completely than the public-facing records suggest.

## Series potential

Yes. A later part could compare the Army's Sourcewell task orders with the Navy's University of Hawaii agreement or trace one service's annual cost-savings certification to installation-level sustainment work.
