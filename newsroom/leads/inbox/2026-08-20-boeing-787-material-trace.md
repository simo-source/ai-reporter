## Hypothesis

The FAA's proposed inspection for certain 787-8 cargo-door split frames may expose a gap between the population covered by the rule and the population reflected in its cost estimate: the rule applies by aircraft type and affected batch, while the estimate counts only four U.S.-registered airplanes.

## What would be new

The new finding would be a document-backed comparison of the FAA's applicability and cost assumptions with the affected batch list, aircraft-registration records, and any later docket or final-rule action—not a recap that the FAA proposed an inspection.

## Primary documents to fetch first

1. Federal Register proposed rule, 2026-17056, especially the Background, Applicability, Costs of Compliance, and incorporated-material sections: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/20/2026-17056/airworthiness-directives-the-boeing-company-airplanes
2. FAA-2026-8784 docket and supporting documents, including Boeing Alert Requirements Bulletin B787-81205-SB530106-00 RB, Issue 001.
3. FAA registry records for the affected U.S.-registered 787-8 aircraft and the final AD, if issued.

## Human stake

Passengers and operators need to know whether the rule's inspection scope and public cost estimate describe the same safety population.

## Why it might be wrong

The four-airplane estimate may be exact if the incorporated Boeing bulletin's batch filter excludes other U.S.-registered aircraft, and the rule may not be intended to quantify worldwide exposure.

Yes. A later part could compare the proposed rule, public comments, and final compliance record if the docket develops.
