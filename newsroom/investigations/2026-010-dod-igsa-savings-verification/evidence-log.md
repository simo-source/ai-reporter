# Evidence log

## E1 — GAO-26-108092

- URL: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108092
- Retrieved: 2026-09-10
- Locator: pages 1–2, 6–7, 16, and appendix I, pages 17–18.
- Excerpt: GAO reports 316 active IGSAs as of December 2025, up from 45 in 2018. It says Army installations spent about $355 million through IGSAs in fiscal year 2025, while Navy, Marine Corps, and Air Force headquarters did not track IGSA spending at headquarters level. GAO reviewed 402 Army Sourcewell task orders and found that 101 either lacked a documented financial benefit or lacked a cost comparison; 49 of those 101 also lacked a documented nonfinancial benefit. It also says the Navy, Marine Corps, and Air Force had no procedures to verify actual IGSA costs and savings after agreements began.
- Boundary: GAO describes selected, nongeneralizable samples; this does not establish that every IGSA lacks records.

## E2 — 10 U.S.C. § 2679

- URL: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title10-section2679&num=0&edition=prelim
- Retrieved: 2026-09-10
- Locator: § 2679(a)(1), (e)(1)–(4), and (f)(1).
- Excerpt: The statute permits an IGSA when the Secretary determines it serves the department's best interests through mission effectiveness or efficiencies, including reduced costs. It requires at least 25 percent of realized cost savings for an installation to be made available to its commander and requires annual certification of the installation, savings achieved, agreement source and type, amount made available, and sustainment, restoration, and modernization uses.
- Boundary: The statute does not show whether any service complied or how realized savings were calculated.

## E3 — DoD Instruction 4000.19

- URL: https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/400019p.pdf
- Retrieved: 2026-09-10
- Locator: sections 1.1(c)–(d), 2.4(c)–(e), 3.2(a)–(d), 3.4(a)–(c), and table 1, requirements 20–22, pages 3–12.
- Excerpt: DoD components must designate agreement managers, establish approval processes, and require internal controls. Cost information must be quantifiable, measurable, and reasonably attributable to the support received. For reimbursable agreements, estimated costs must be reviewed annually to identify current-year actual costs.
- Boundary: This instruction is not the IGSA-specific annual certification requirement in § 2679(e)(3), and its annual cost review does not prove realized savings were verified.

## E4 — Army Regulation 5-9

- URL: https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/pdf/web/ARN3159_AR5-9_Web_FINAL.pdf
- Retrieved: 2026-09-11
- Locator: chapter 8, paragraphs 8-5(b)(4)–(5), 8-6(d)–(e), 8-8(a)–(b), PDF pages 38–40.
- Excerpt: Army policy assigns IGSA program oversight, twice-yearly reporting to senior leaders, OSD, and Congress, quarterly reporting by installation providers, a cost-benefit analysis for each proposal, an approval letter, delivery of the completed agreement within 90 days, six-year document retention, and separate IGSA manager and technical-representative roles.
- Boundary: The regulation states what records and controls should exist; it does not show that public reports disclose realized savings or the statutory installation-level recapture and uses.

## E5 — Fort Leonard Wood lawn-care IGSA announcement

- URL: https://www.army.mil/article/293522/making_the_cut_new_partnership_ensures_consistent_fort_leonard_wood_lawn_care
- Retrieved: 2026-09-11
- Locator: paragraphs beginning “A new Intergovernmental Support Agreement is now active,” “Under the new lawn care IGSA,” and “The new grounds maintenance agreement.”
- Excerpt: The Army says the lawn-care IGSA began June 1, 2026, covers more than 1,600 acres, is renewable for up to nine years, and is one of nine active Fort Leonard Wood IGSAs. It reports approximately $2.9 million in annual cost savings and cost avoidances across those agreements.
- Boundary: The announcement does not identify the nine agreements, separate projected from realized savings, provide a cost-benefit analysis or approval letter, quantify the 25-percent installation-level amount, or identify sustainment, restoration, and modernization uses.
