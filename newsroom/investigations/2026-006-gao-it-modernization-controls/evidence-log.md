# Evidence log

## E1 — GAO OIG-26-2, Information Technology Modernization

- URL: https://www.gao.gov/products/oig-26-2
- Retrieved: 2026-08-31
- Locator: “Fast Facts,” “What the OIG Found,” and “Recommendations.”
- Captured excerpt: GAO says it paid $1 million in 2021 for a five-year plan with initiatives projected at about $29 million; less than 18 months later it moved to an ongoing strategy, and the decision was undocumented. As of May 2026, 39 of 61 initiatives were reported complete, while some smaller initiatives were tracked under larger projects, making actual costs for all initiatives difficult to determine.
- Why it matters: Establishes the current audit baseline and the specific cost/progress join that the other records must test.

## E3 — GAO-26-900644, Performance and Accountability Report, FY2025

- URL: https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-900644.pdf

## E4 — GAO-25-900618, Fiscal Year 2026 Performance Plan

- URL: https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-25-900618.pdf
- Retrieved: 2026-08-31
- Locator: “Challenge: Managing Our IT Systems,” pp. 14–15.
- Captured excerpt: GAO says cloud migration and network modernization begun in FY2024 were ongoing, it was replacing its legacy document-management system with an enterprise-content-management solution, it implemented nine automated workflow applications in FY2024, and primary IT support services would shift to a new contract in FY2025.
- Why it matters: Adds concrete initiative examples and a contract transition, but it still does not map costs or outcomes to the OIG’s 61-initiative count.

## Interpretation boundary

The records now expose more concrete work than the OIG summary alone: nine automated workflow applications, cloud/network migration, an enterprise-content-management replacement, and a support-contract shift. They still do not reconcile those examples to the 61-initiative count, the $29 million plan estimate, or the later broad deferral categories. This supports a research finding about public-reporting granularity, not a claim of waste, failed delivery, or improper spending.
