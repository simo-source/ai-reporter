# Assignment

## Hypothesis

DOD's rapid expansion of intergovernmental support agreements may have outpaced the records needed to verify realized savings and calculate the amounts that the statute requires the services to return to installations for sustainment, restoration, and modernization.

## Primary documents

1. GAO-26-108092, *DOD Installation Services: Action Needed to Improve Oversight of Intergovernmental Support Agreements*:
   https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108092
2. 10 U.S.C. § 2679, *Installation-support services: intergovernmental support agreements*:
   https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title10-section2679&num=0&edition=prelim
3. DoD Instruction 4000.19, *Support Agreements*:
   https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/400019p.pdf
4. Service-level IGSA approvals, cost-benefit analyses, task-order records, and the annual certifications required by 10 U.S.C. § 2679(e)(3).

## What would be new

The finding would map the statutory cost-savings certification and recapture requirements against service-level guidance and public agreement, task-order, or certification records, showing whether claimed savings can be independently tied to required installation-level amounts.

## Human stake

The records govern how public money is spent on installation services and whether verified savings return to local military installations for maintenance and modernization.

## Why it might be wrong

The GAO review uses selected, nongeneralizable samples, and service records not yet located may document the required certifications and actual savings more completely than public-facing records suggest.

## Series potential

Yes. A later part could compare the Army's Sourcewell task orders with the Navy's University of Hawaii agreement or trace one service's annual cost-savings certification to installation-level sustainment work.
