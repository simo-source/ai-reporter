## Hypothesis

The Navy's FY2026 maintenance funding and shipyard-capacity response may not yet demonstrate recovery of the attack-submarine maintenance backlog: the public record may show a persistent class-level gap between the Navy's stated 20% maintenance benchmark and the number of SSNs in depot maintenance or awaiting maintenance.

## What would be new

The new finding would calculate the class-level gap against the Navy's benchmark and test it against later Navy, NAVSEA, congressional, or budget-execution records showing whether capacity investments produced measurable availability or idle-day improvements. It would not equate resource days, combat-surge readiness, or procurement cadence with attack-submarine availability.

## Human stake

Maintenance delays can keep crews and nuclear-powered attack submarines unavailable while public funds support the force and its shipyards.

## Why it might be wrong

The CRS table is historical and combines Navy-provided data with a broad maintenance category; it does not by itself establish current FY2026 performance or causation. Classified or internal readiness records may contain the milestones missing from public documents.
