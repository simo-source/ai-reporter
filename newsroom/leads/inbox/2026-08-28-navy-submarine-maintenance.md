# Lead: Navy attack-submarine maintenance promises and operating evidence

## Scores

- Public interest: 5/5
- Originality: 3/5

- Room to grow: 5/5
- Legal risk (subtracted): 1/5

- Score note: Two independent primary records create a testable comparison rather than a single-report recap. Originality is raised to 3 because the Navy budget describes its FY2026 maintenance and readiness targets while GAO measures the prior decade's lost operational days and associated sustaining cost; the comparison is not stated in either source's summary.

## Hypothesis

The Navy's FY2026 maintenance funding and on-time-readiness targets may not yet provide a public, class-level way to determine whether attack-submarine maintenance delays are being reduced.

## What would be new

The new finding would reconcile GAO's historical attack-submarine operational-day losses and sustaining costs with the Navy's FY2026 ship-maintenance funding, OPTEMPO, shipyard workload, and stated maintenance-delay targets, then test whether the public budget identifies measurable recovery milestones.

## Primary excerpts captured

- GAO product intake, What GAO Found: over the last 10 years the Navy lost more than 15,000 operational days on active attack submarines, with an estimated $3.4 billion spent sustaining crews and submarines that supplied no operational capability. Locator: GAO-26-109256 product record and summary, https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-109256.
- Navy FY2026 President's Budget Highlights, pp. 1–20, readiness overview and ship operations: the request emphasizes ship maintenance, provides for 288 battle-force ships, and maintains 58 underway days per quarter for deployed forces and 24 for non-deployed forces.
- Navy FY2026 President's Budget, Operation and Maintenance, Navy, Naval Shipyards section, performance data: total mission resource days for submarines are projected at 538,208 in FY2026, compared with 539,045 in FY2025; the table separately reports shipyard workload, not the GAO attack-submarine operational-day loss measure.

## Human stake

Maintenance delays can keep crews and nuclear-powered attack submarines unavailable while public funds continue to support them.

## Why it might be wrong

The measures may cover different populations, time periods, or accounting definitions. The Navy's classified or internal readiness data may also contain the class-level milestones missing from public budget books, making a public comparison incomplete rather than evidence of a policy failure.

## Series potential

Yes. A series could compare public maintenance targets with shipyard workload and later GAO, Navy, or congressional oversight records, while keeping operational-day and resource-day definitions separate.
