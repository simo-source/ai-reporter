NASA's own annual accountability report says 94 recommendations from the Government Accountability Office (GAO) and NASA's inspector general were open for at least one year as of December 31, 2025. The total covers 48 public reports, including 29 GAO recommendations and 65 from the NASA Office of Inspector General. [c1]

The report places two GAO recommendations in its “Open, Will not Implement” section. One, from GAO-20-68, asks NASA to create a life-cycle cost estimate for the Artemis III mission. NASA's stated rationale is that it tracks Artemis costs by major component or element rather than by mission or flight. [c2, c3]

The other, from GAO-24-106624, asks NASA leadership to develop an implementation plan with timelines for updating spacecraft acquisition policies and standards with essential cyber controls. NASA says it partially concurred with the recommendation but did not agree that the named officials needed to create that plan. [c4]

The two entries are part of NASA's report on recommendations issued through December 31, 2024, that remained unimplemented for at least a year. NASA defines “open” to include actions pending, in progress, completed but awaiting auditor verification, or recommendations the agency decided not to implement.

The current GAO reports feed supplies a separate status update. It says GAO identified three NASA priority recommendations in August 2025 and that NASA had not implemented any of them; it says an additional priority recommendation was identified in July 2026, bringing the total to four. The feed highlights program-cost monitoring, cybersecurity risk management, and federal-contracting metrics. [c5] The feed does not identify the recommendation numbers, so this article does not claim that the two NASA entries above are the current fourth item.

These records establish recommendation status and NASA's stated rationales. They do not, by themselves, establish intent, fraud, negligence, or a quantified operational consequence.
