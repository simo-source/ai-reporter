# Assignment: Federal uranium supply cost visibility

## Hypothesis

Federal uranium-supply programs may have committed substantial public funding before DOE documented the economic analysis, strategic plan, and schedule needed to show that the awards are sized to create a sustainable domestic enrichment market.

## What would be new

The new finding would identify a specific mismatch between DOE's award milestones or expected production and the assumptions, deliverables, or costs in the underlying HALEU/LEU program records—not simply repeat GAO's conclusion that documentation is incomplete.

## Primary documents to fetch first

1. GAO-26-107385, Nuclear Fuel: https://files.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-107385.pdf
2. DOE HALEU enrichment services program page: https://www.energy.gov/ne/haleu-enrichment-services
3. DOE report to Congress on HALEU availability and the task-order solicitations or award documents cited by GAO.

## Human stake

Federal uranium-supply choices affect nuclear security, reactor fuel availability, and billions of dollars in public spending.

## Why it might be wrong

The missing DOE plan or economic analysis may be available in a not-yet-linked public document, or the award milestones may provide a defensible proxy even without a consolidated program plan.

Yes. A later part could test one award's milestones, production assumptions, or conversion dependency against DOE's supporting record.
