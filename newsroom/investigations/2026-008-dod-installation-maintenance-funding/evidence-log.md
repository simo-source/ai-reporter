# Evidence log

## E1 — GAO-26-107255, Installation Maintenance

- URL: https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107255/index.html
- Retrieved: 2026-09-03
- Filename: official GAO accessible report text
- Locator: Highlights, “What GAO Found”; Letter, “DOD Has Not Requested Full Funding for Maintenance Activities,” paragraphs 134–160.
- Captured excerpt: GAO says DOD's deferred maintenance increased from $137 billion in FY2020 to an estimated $285 billion in FY2025. It says the 2014 policy goal was to fund regular maintenance at 90 percent or higher of Facilities Sustainment Model recommendations; for FY2021–FY2025, the Army, Navy, and Air Force generally requested around 80 percent, while Marine Corps requests fell from 80 percent to 40 percent. GAO also reports that none of the eight contacted installations received funding meeting the 90-percent goal.
- Boundary: GAO says the eight-installation sample was purposeful and non-generalizable; the site figures are anecdotal evidence, not a department-wide estimate.

## E2 — DOD FY2026 Budget Request Overview Book

- URL: https://comptroller.defense.gov/Portals/45/Documents/defbudget/FY2026/FY2026_Budget_Request_Overview_Book.pdf
- Retrieved: 2026-09-03
- Filename: official DOD budget overview PDF text
- Locator: “FY 2026 Budget Request” overview, facilities-investment summary; Department of the Navy and Army National Guard budget-request sections.
- Captured excerpt: DOD's overview requests $27.3 billion for Facilities Sustainment, Restoration, and Modernization and describes sustainment at an 85-percent minimum and 87-percent average. The Navy section separately reports a $5.7 billion FY2026 FSRM request, while the Army National Guard section says its request funds facility sustainment at 85 percent and base-operations support at 90 percent.
- Boundary: The overview does not, in the captured sections, explain whether the department-wide percentages cover the same requirement population, services, or measure as GAO's FY2021–FY2025 comparisons.

## E4 — Army FY2026 Operation and Maintenance justification

- URL: https://www.asafm.army.mil/Portals/72/Documents/BudgetMaterial/2026/Discretionary%20Budget/Operation%20and%20Maintenance/Regular%20Army%20Operation%20and%20Maintenance%20Volume%201.pdf
- Retrieved: 2026-09-04
- Locator: Introductory Statement, Exhibit PBA-19, page 6; Detail by Subactivity Group 132, Exhibit OP-5, page 247; “Funding Increases and Decreases.”
- Captured excerpt: The Army says its FY2026 facility-sustainment request maintains funding at 91 percent of the Department of Defense Facility Sustainment Model and funds barracks and soldier housing at 100 percent. The OP-5 detail lists $6,159.744 million discretionary and $427.213 million mandatory reconciliation funding, for $6,586.957 million total. It describes a $299.607 million increase for real-property maintenance and says the funding is intended to maintain 91 percent of the model and improve the repair backlog.
- Boundary: This is a budget request and justification, not an FY2026 obligation, work-order closure, or measured backlog result. Its 91-percent statement applies to the Army regular component as described in the book, not automatically to every DOD component.

## E5 — Navy FY2026 Operation and Maintenance justification

- URL: https://www.secnav.navy.mil/fmc/fmb/Documents/26pres/OMN_Book.pdf
- Retrieved: 2026-09-04
- Locator: Appropriation Highlights, “Facilities Sustainment Restoration and Modernization”; Base Support, Subactivity Group BSM1, Exhibit OP-5, page 408.
- Captured excerpt: The Navy says the FY2026 request includes $3,991.438 million discretionary and $1,686.916 million mandatory reconciliation funding, for $5,678.354 million total in BSM1. It says the mandatory amount provides facility sustainment at 85 percent of the modeled requirement, poor or failing-condition unaccompanied-housing recapitalization, and supplements the Navy’s restoration and modernization backlog. The highlights identify a $475 billion plant replacement value against which the Navy says it faces a facilities-maintenance backlog.
- Boundary: The 85-percent statement is conditional on enactment of the reconciliation bill and concerns the Navy’s modeled requirement; the book does not by itself quantify FY2026 obligations, completed work orders, or backlog change.

## E6 — Marine Corps Reserve FY2026 Operation and Maintenance justification

- URL: https://www.secnav.navy.mil/fmc/fmb/Documents/26pres/OMMCR_Book.pdf
- Retrieved: 2026-09-04
- Locator: Appropriation Highlights, pages 5–6; Base Support, Subactivity Group BSM1, Exhibit OP-5, pages 35–36; “Performance Criteria and Evaluation Summary.”
- Captured excerpt: The book states that the Marine Corps Reserve FY2026 estimate funds facility sustainment at 53 percent of the OSD model. Its performance table lists a $41.846 million FY2026 sustainment requirement, $21.793 million sustainment funding, $0.489 million military-pay sustainment, and $22.282 million total sustainment funding; it reports “% Sustainment of FSM 26.2” as 53 percent. The same exhibit says the FSRM subactivity funds the Reserve headquarters and all reserve installations.
- Boundary: This is a Reserve-component budget request, not the active Marine Corps or the whole Department of Defense. The 53-percent figure is a component-specific comparison point and cannot by itself contradict DOD’s department-wide 85-percent minimum/87-percent average framing.

## E7 — DOD Financial Management Regulation, Volume 2B, Chapter 8

- URL: https://comptroller.defense.gov/Portals/45/documents/fmr/current/02b/02b_08.pdf
- Retrieved: 2026-09-04
- Locator: Sections 2.1.2–2.1.7 and 2.2.1, pages 8-8 through 8-9.
- Captured excerpt: The regulation defines FSRM to include recurring maintenance and repair, real-property renovation, and associated planning and design. It requires component justification levels to correspond to Facilities Data Quality Assurance program elements and directs components to account for military-personnel, host-nation, non-federal domestic, Defense Working Capital Fund, and RDT&E contributions that support facility sustainment.
- Boundary: This is a budgeting and reporting rule, not evidence that any component met a modeled requirement or reduced a backlog. It helps identify possible denominator differences but does not resolve them.
