## Hypothesis

DOE's contractor-assurance system may be formally required but operationally weak because contract incentives and performance evaluations do not translate CAS performance into clear, measurable consequences.

## What would be new

The finding would compare DOE's written CAS requirements with the selected contracts' fee structures, performance-measurement plans, and documented fee decisions to determine whether the accountability mechanism described in policy operated in practice.

## Human stake

DOE's Office of Environmental Management estimates that its remaining nuclear cleanup work could cost $641 billion to $840 billion, making reliable contractor oversight material to public safety, schedule, and taxpayer exposure.

## Why it might be wrong

The GAO report used a nongeneralizable sample, and the underlying contracts or later DOE records may show that fee mechanisms and oversight controls work in ways not visible in the report's selected examples.

## Series potential

Yes. A series could test CAS requirements and fee accountability at each selected site, then compare any later DOE response with the original contract record.
